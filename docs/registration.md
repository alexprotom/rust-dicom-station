# Image registration

Rigid and deformable (B-spline) intensity-based registration between the
two loaded datasets, following the [elastix](https://elastix.dev)
framework. elastix itself is a C++/ITK toolbox, so its core algorithms are
**re-implemented natively in Rust** to keep the application
single-language.

![registration](screenshot_registration.png)

*Deformable registration of two breathing phases: the sidebar reports the

aligned anatomy in gray, residual respiratory mismatch as magenta/green
fringes.*

## Algorithms

* **Multi-resolution Gaussian pyramids** (`NumberOfResolutions`,
  default 3): repeated [1 2 1]/4 smoothing + factor-2 decimation with
  correct voxel-center origin bookkeeping.
* **Random coordinate sampling** with fresh samples every iteration
  (`NumberOfSpatialSamples`, default 3000), restricted to a body mask from
  a configurable HU threshold (default −500). Sampling uses a
  deterministic, seedable XorShift stream, so runs are reproducible.
* **Metric:** mean squared HU difference with analytic gradients
  (appropriate for mono-modal CT–CT alignment; mutual information for
  CT–MR is a natural extension).
* **Optimizer: Adaptive Stochastic Gradient Descent** (Klein et al., IJCV
  2009 — elastix's default) with automatic gain estimation, the sigmoid
  time-adaptation rule and a trust-region step cap.
* **Rigid:** 6-DOF Euler transform about the fixed-image center, with
  automatic rotation/translation parameter scaling.
* **Deformable:** the recovered rigid transform composed with a cubic
  B-spline free-form deformation on a regular grid
  (`FinalGridSpacingInPhysicalUnits`, default 32 mm), optimized
  coarse-to-fine across the pyramid.

The transform convention is **fixed → moving** patient coordinates; the
inverse (needed for the crosshair link) is computed exactly for the rigid
part and by fixed-point iteration for the deformable part.

## Running a registration

With two datasets loaded, the *Registration* menu or sidebar section
registers one dataset onto the other — the direction is selectable
(**B ▶ A** or **A ▶ B**; the second-named dataset is the fixed image and
receives the fusion overlay). Iterations per level, samples per iteration
and the B-spline grid spacing are adjustable in the sidebar. Registration
runs on a background thread with progress display and a **Cancel** button;
thanks to stochastic sampling a typical run takes seconds.

On the bundled data (512 × 512 × 133 CT, two breathing phases): rigid
pre-alignment plus three B-spline resolution levels, 1800 iterations
total, ≈ 20 s on a desktop CPU, driving the mean-squared HU difference
from ≈ 9700 to ≈ 1800. The residual magenta/green fringes at the diaphragm
and chest wall mark exactly the respiratory motion a 32 mm grid cannot
fully model — tightening the grid refines it at the cost of runtime.

## The fusion overlay and crosshair mapping

The result panel reports direction, metric before/after, the recovered
translation/rotation and timing, and enables a **magenta/green fusion**
on the fixed study: fixed image in magenta, transformed moving image in
green — aligned anatomy reads gray — with a blend slider. The cross-study
crosshair link maps through the recovered transform (inverse included), so
clicking a point in either study lands on the same anatomy in the other.

A rigid matrix from a DICOM **REG** object can be applied instead of
running the optimizer — see [rt-objects.md](rt-objects.md).

## Transform simulator (registration QA)

The *Simulation* sidebar section applies an **exactly known** transform to
a loaded dataset — rigid motion (translation + Euler rotation about the
volume center) plus an optional local Gaussian deformation (amplitude
vector + σ, centered at the crosshair) — and generates the result into the
other dataset slot: the CT is resampled through the inverse transform, and
structure contours, dose grids and plan isocenters are carried along. The
applied parameters stay displayed as ground truth, so you can immediately
run the built-in registration and compare the recovered transform against
it. Any dataset — original or simulated — can then be exported as DICOM
(see [export-and-tools.md](export-and-tools.md)).

## Accuracy verification

`tests/registration.rs` registers analytically known transforms on a
synthetic phantom: the recovered mapping lands within ≈ 0.6 mm of a known
rigid rotation + translation and within ≈ 0.3 mm of a 7 mm Gaussian-bump
deformation (asserted tolerances 1.5 mm and 3 mm; inverse round-trips are
verified to sub-voxel precision). On the synthetic phantom generated by
the built-in test-data generator, rigid recovery matches the simulated
ground truth to sub-millimeter/sub-degree.

## Notes

Deformable results are intensity-driven: displacements inside large
uniform regions are interpolated from the B-spline grid rather than
measured. The MSD metric assumes comparable intensities (CT–CT); for
CT–MR, expect to need a mutual-information metric before results are
meaningful.
